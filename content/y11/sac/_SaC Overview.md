# Society and Culture

> [!Warning]
> To avoid copyright issues, definitions from the syllabus have been paraphrased. However, they should still have the same meaning or connotations. 
> 
> If you need *exact* syllabus definitions, you can refer to the syllabus linked below.

Society and Culture is a course designed to encourage [[#Social and Cultural Literacy|literacy]] for individuals in both the societal and cultural aspects. Through each prescribed depth study and the exploration of concepts such as persons, culture, time, society, and environment students can further understand how not only does society change and grow across time, but also detach from the idea of [[cultural relativism]].

## Social and Cultural Literacy
Social and cultural literacy refers to the possession of skills, knowledge, and understanding for effective communication and respect towards others and themselves. This is through a student’s achievement of synthesis between [[#Personal Experience and Public Knowledge|personal experience and public knowledge]] at the [[#Micro, Meso, and Macro|micro, meso, and macro levels]] of society.

Being able to be socially and culturally literate means that one will possess the following characteristics, which include but are not limited to:
- Having a sense of personal, social, and cultural identity, understanding the culture that underpins their values, beliefs, and behaviour
- Having the ability to empathise with and appreciate diverse values and beliefs of different societies and cultures
- Effectively and ethically researches, illustrating critical discernment regarding information and the media
- Is able to effectively communicate with individuals and groups, working cooperatively in cross-cultural settings
- Avoids being [[cultural relativism|culturally relative]], such as judging another culture’s practices using their own values from their own culture
- *And more, which you can find on the syllabus!*

### Personal Experience and Public Knowledge
Personal experience is the knowledge gained from the reflection of individual experiences. It is incredibly important in this course, as it allows for the achievement of [[#Social and Cultural Literacy|social and cultural literacy]].

Public knowledge is the general knowledge and knowledge under the public domain, meaning it is accessible to everyone. Work or research from other people under the public domain is considered as public knowledge.

Combining both personal experience and public knowledge allows for a holistic and balanced understanding of [[society]] and [[culture]], achieving [[#Social and Cultural Literacy|social and cultural literacy]].
