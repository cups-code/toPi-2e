# The Chain Rule
## Ref Sheet Formulae
$$
y=f(x)^n -> "dy"/"dx"=n f'(x)[f(x)]^(n-1) \
y=g(u), "where" u=f(x) -> "dy"/"dx"="dy"/"du" times "du"/"dx"
$$

## Other Formulae
$$
h(x)=f(g(x)) -> h'(x)=f'(g(x)) dot g'(x) \
"dy"/"dx" = "dy"/"du" dot "du"/"dx"
$$

> [!Abstract] Explanation
> In layman’s terms, you basically:
> - Bring down the magnitude of n
> - Multiply f(x) by n
> - Multiply the equation by f’(x)

> [!Example] Example: $y=4/(sqrt(5x-2))$ in terms of $"dy"/"dx"$ from rough generalisation
> $$
> y=4/(sqrt(5x-2)) \
> y=4(5x-2)^(-1/2) \
> ("dy")/("dx")=4 times -1/2 times (5x-2)^(-3/2) times 5 \
> ("dy")/("dx")=-2(5x-2)^(-3/2) times 5 \
> ("dy")/("dx")=-10(5x-2)^(-3/2) \
> ("dy")/("dx")=-10 times 1/(sqrt((5x-2)^3)) \
> therefore ("dy")/("dx")=-10/(sqrt((5x-2)^3)
> $$

> [!Example] Example $y=(x^3+2)^5$ with respect to $"dy"/"dx"$ using formula
> > [!Abstract] Source
> > This example was found from an Atomi module called “The Chain Rule”.
> 
> $$
> "dy"/"dx"="dy"/"du" times "du"/"dx" \
> "Let" y=u^5, u=x^3+2 space "(u = inner function)" \
> "dy"/"du"="d"/"du" times (u)^5 \
> "(Solve for " "dy"/"du" space "and" "du"/"dx" ")" \
> "dy"/"du"=5 u^4 \
> "du"/"dx"="d"/"dx" times (x^3 + 2) = 3x^2 \
> "dy"/"dx"=5 u^4 times 3x^2 \
> "dy"/"dx"=(5 u^4)(3x^2) \
> "dy"/"dx"=15x^2u^4 \
> "(Sub u into " "dy"/"dx" space "equation)" \
> "dy"/"dx"=15x^2(x^3+2)^4
> $$
