# Logarithms
Logarithms are the reverse operation of exponentials. It is utilised to find the power of the base of an exponential.
$$
B^P=A \
log_B A = P
$$
This is where $B$ is the base, $P$ is the power, and $A$ is the answer.

| Exponential Form: $B^P=A$ | Logarithmic Form: $log_B A=P$ |
| ------------------------- | ----------------------------- |
| $2^3=8$                   | $log_2 8 = 3$                 |
| $2^10=1024$               | $log_2 100 = 2$               |
| $2^10=1024$               | $log_2 1024 = 10$             |

## Properties of Logarithms
$$
log_a a=1 \
log_a 1 = 0 \
log_a a^x = x \
a^(log_a x)=x 
$$

> [!Tip]
> You can view $log_a a^x = x$ as $log_a$ and $a$ cancelling each other.

## Logarithms in Relation to Exponential Functions

> [!Tip] Related
> See [[E1. Introduction to Exponential Functions]].

$$
"Let" y=a^x, "thus" x=log_a y
$$
The same properties from exponential functions (e.g. $a>0$) also apply to logarithms. $a$ can also not equal to 1. See [[E1. Introduction to Exponential Functions]] for every property.
